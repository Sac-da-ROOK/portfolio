# Predator vs. Prey: A Balance of Nature

Predators and prey are two important parts of many ecosystems.

A predator hunts another animal for food, while the animal being hunted is called the prey.

These relationships might seem simple, but they can have a huge effect on an entire ecosystem.

## How Predator and Prey Populations Connect

Predator and prey populations are connected to each other.

If there are many prey animals, predators usually have more food available.

This can allow the predator population to increase.

But if there are too many predators, they may eat more prey than the ecosystem can replace.

The prey population can then decrease.

With fewer prey available, the predator population may eventually decrease too.

This can create a cycle between the two populations.

## Why Predators Are Important

Predators help control prey populations.

Without predators, some prey populations could grow very quickly.

A large prey population could then consume too many plants or other resources.

This can put pressure on other organisms in the food web.

Predators can therefore affect parts of an ecosystem far beyond the animals they directly hunt.

## What Happens When Predators Disappear?

When an important predator disappears, the effects can spread through the ecosystem.

For example, if a predator that eats herbivores disappears, the herbivore population could increase.

The larger herbivore population might eat more vegetation.

With less vegetation, other animals that depend on those plants could also be affected.

This type of chain reaction is called a **trophic cascade**.

## A Real Example

Sea otters provide an interesting example.

Sea otters eat sea urchins.

When sea otter populations became very small, sea urchin populations could increase.

The sea urchins then ate more kelp.

Kelp forests provide food and shelter for many organisms, so losing large amounts of kelp affected other parts of the ecosystem too.

This shows how the removal of one predator can affect organisms much farther down the food web.

## Balance Does Not Mean Everything Stays the Same

An ecosystem is not perfectly balanced all the time.

Populations naturally increase and decrease.

Weather, food availability, disease, competition, and other factors can change population sizes.

The important part is that these changes are connected.

A change in one population can create changes in others.

## Why Food Webs Matter

Predator-prey relationships are only one part of a larger food web.

A predator may eat several different prey species.

A prey species may also be eaten by several different predators.

Because of these connections, ecosystems can be complicated.

Changing one population can sometimes affect many others.

### Reflection

Learning about predator and prey relationships made me realize that predators aren't just animals that hunt other animals.

They can help control populations and influence the rest of an ecosystem.

The biggest thing I learned is that nature is connected through many different relationships.

**A change between one predator and its prey can sometimes create changes throughout an entire food web.**
