**🔭 Why Metal Conducts Electricity**

When you plug in a device, electricity usually travels through metal wires. Copper is commonly used in electrical wiring because it allows electric charge to move through it easily.

But why does metal conduct electricity so well?

The answer has to do with the way atoms are arranged and how electrons behave inside the material.

**What Is Inside an Atom?**

Atoms contain a nucleus made of protons and neutrons. Electrons move around the nucleus.

The electrons in an atom do not all behave exactly the same way. Some are held tightly by the atom, while others are farther from the nucleus and can move more easily.

In metals, some electrons are not strongly attached to individual atoms. Instead, they can move through the material.

These are often called **free electrons**.

**A Sea of Electrons**

A useful way to imagine a metal is as a group of positive metal ions surrounded by a sea of moving electrons.

The metal ions stay arranged in a solid structure, while some electrons can move throughout the material.

This is different from many other materials, where electrons are held more tightly in place.

Because metals have mobile electrons, electric charge can move through them relatively easily. ([open.edu](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/science-maths-technology/physics-and-maths/physics/how-do-batteries-work/content-section-2))

**What Happens When a Battery Is Connected?**

Imagine connecting a copper wire to a battery.

The battery creates an electric potential difference between its terminals. This produces an electric field that affects the electrons inside the wire.

The electrons begin to move in a general direction through the wire.

Their movement creates an electric current.

The electrons do not necessarily travel from the battery to the device instantly. Instead, the electric field spreads through the circuit, causing electrons already inside the wire to begin moving.

This is one reason a light turns on almost immediately after you close a circuit.

**Why Copper Is Used So Often**

Copper is a very useful material for wires because it has several important properties.

- It conducts electricity well.
- It can be drawn into thin wires.
- It is flexible enough for many applications.
- It is relatively easy to work with.
- It is more affordable than some other highly conductive metals.

Aluminum is also used in electrical systems, especially when lighter wires are useful.

Silver conducts electricity even better than copper, but it is much more expensive, so it is not normally used for ordinary household wiring.

**Conductors vs. Insulators**

Materials that allow electric charge to move easily are called **conductors**.

Examples include:

- Copper
- Aluminum
- Silver
- Gold
- Many other metals

Materials that strongly resist the movement of electric charge are called **insulators**.

Examples include:

- Rubber
- Plastic
- Glass
- Dry wood
- Ceramic

Insulators are also important because they can surround wires and help prevent unwanted electric current from reaching people or other objects.

**Why Doesn't Plastic Conduct Electricity Like Metal?**

Plastic usually does not have many electrons that can move freely throughout the material.

Its electrons are generally held more tightly within atoms or chemical bonds.

Because there are fewer mobile charges, electric current does not move through plastic easily.

That is why electrical wires often have metal inside and plastic or rubber outside.

The metal carries the current, while the outer material helps protect people from the current.

**Does Electricity Move Through Every Metal the Same Way?**

No.

Different metals have different electrical properties. Some allow charge to move more easily than others.

The resistance of a wire depends on several factors, including:

- The type of material
- The length of the wire
- The thickness of the wire
- The temperature

A longer wire usually has more resistance than a shorter wire made from the same material.

A thicker wire usually has less resistance than a thinner wire made from the same material.

This is why engineers must choose the correct wire for a particular electrical system.

**What Happens When a Wire Gets Hot?**

In many metals, resistance increases as temperature rises.

As the metal gets hotter, the atoms in its structure vibrate more. These vibrations can make it harder for moving electrons to travel through the material.

More resistance can cause electrical energy to be converted into heat.

This is useful in devices such as electric heaters and toasters, but too much heat in an ordinary wire can be dangerous.

**Why Metal Conductivity Matters**

Metal conductivity is one of the reasons modern technology is possible.

Electrical wires connect power plants, homes, computers, lights, machines, and many other devices.

Without materials that allow electric charge to move, electrical circuits would be much harder to build.

The simple copper wire in a circuit may not look very impressive, but it depends on the behavior of enormous numbers of electrons.

Copper wires work because their structure allows electric charge to move relatively easily. Plastic is used around the wire because it does the opposite and resists current.
